0188: ### Background and Rationale
0190: `lastking` matters because it is trying to build a defense game around pressure, planning, and deterministic accountability rather than around raw action density or loosely tuned sandbox chaos. The PRD defines a very specific promise: a 15-day survival structure where daytime preparation and nighttime validation form the heart of the experience.
0192: The current repository state shows that a large part of this promise already exists at the systems and governance level. Day/night state flow, wave channels, budget logic, economy rules, save handling, configuration safety, and validation infrastructure are substantially stronger than the current player-facing presentation. That makes this project important now: the next major value is no longer “invent the rules,” but “make the existing rules legible, playable, and coherent for an actual player.”
0194: From a development perspective, this project also represents a disciplined alternative to ad hoc prototype growth. It combines game rules, config contracts, auditability, and UI wiring under one governed workflow. That is a meaningful differentiator for a solo or small-team strategy project, because it reduces drift while making future balancing and expansion more defensible.
0198: ## Unique Selling Points (USPs)
0200: - **Daytime planning and nighttime proof under locked deterministic rules**
0201: Many defense games create tension through volume and spectacle. `lastking` instead emphasizes whether your daytime economic, building, recruitment, and tech decisions actually hold up under a fixed nighttime validation loop.
0203: - **System-governed difficulty, spawning, and auditability**
0204: Core runtime behavior is not treated as loose tuning data. Difficulty, spawn composition, pressure scoring, migration, and config safety are all governed by explicit contracts and validation paths, giving the game a stronger “designed and reviewable system” identity.
0206: - **Castle-defense loop with economy debt pressure instead of pure expansion fantasy**
0207: The game’s rules around debt, refund, queue interruption, night pressure, and victory/failure produce a harsher planning space than a conventional relaxed builder. The player is not simply optimizing growth; they are making survivability tradeoffs under locked constraints.
0209: - **M1 designed as a truthful vertical slice, not a fake feature-complete façade**
0210: The current milestone is valuable because it exposes the real state of the game: strong systems, partial surfaces, and specific wiring gaps. That honesty supports faster iteration and better design correction than pretending the experience is already complete.
0212: ### Competitive Positioning
0214: `lastking` is best positioned as a midcore single-player PC tower-defense/strategy hybrid for players who want stronger rule clarity, higher systemic accountability, and longer-form run pressure than casual tower defense games usually provide.
0216: It does not primarily compete on spectacle, narrative character fantasy, or broad accessibility-first reach. It competes on the quality of its survival loop, the legibility of its planning consequences, and the credibility of its deterministic systems. If fully realized, it can stand out as a more governed, pressure-focused, systems-heavy alternative inside the PC defense niche.
0220: ## Core Gameplay
0222: ### Game Pillars
0224: - **Prepared Survival Over Reactive Chaos**
0225: The game is about whether the player’s daytime preparation can survive nighttime pressure, not about last-second twitch recovery or high-APM control.
0227: - **Deterministic Pressure With Legible Consequences**
0228: Waves, economy, targeting, rewards, and failure states should feel strict but understandable. Players should be able to see that outcomes follow rules, not arbitrary spikes.
0230: - **Strategic Tradeoffs Across a 15-Day Arc**
0231: Every build, upgrade, recruit, repair, and tech choice must compete for limited resources and future survivability. Short-term relief should often cost long-term resilience.
0233: - **Readable Systems Before Spectacle**
0234: UI, feedback, and flow should help players understand state, pressure, and consequences. If a system exists but the player cannot read it, the design is still incomplete.
0236: **Pillar Prioritization:** When pillars conflict, prioritize in this order:
0237: 1. Prepared Survival Over Reactive Chaos
0238: 2. Deterministic Pressure With Legible Consequences
0239: 3. Strategic Tradeoffs Across a 15-Day Arc
0240: 4. Readable Systems Before Spectacle
0242: ### Core Gameplay Loop
0244: The core loop of `lastking` is a repeating day-night survival cycle:
0246: **DayStart -> BuildAndRecruit -> NightPrepare -> NightCombat -> Settlement -> RewardChoice -> NextDay**
0248: During the day, the player stabilizes economy, spends resources, places or upgrades structures, manages training queues, and makes progression decisions. During night preparation, the game locks in the coming pressure phase. During night combat, the player watches the consequences of earlier planning play out under deterministic wave and targeting rules. Settlement then summarizes losses and gains, followed by a reward choice that slightly reshapes the next day’s options. The next day begins under tighter pressure, repeating the cycle until the run ends.
0250: **Loop Diagram:**
0251: `Income/Autosave -> Build/Upgrade/Train -> Lock Night State -> Defend Against Wave -> Resolve Damage/Losses -> Pick Reward -> Start Next Day`
0253: **Loop Timing:**
0254: - One macro loop: `Day 4 minutes + Night 2 minutes`
0255: - One full run target: `Day 1 ~ Day 15`
0256: - Intended run length: `60-90 minutes`
0258: **Loop Variation:**
0259: Each iteration changes because of:
0260: - escalating day-based pressure and budget scaling
0261: - elite and boss night structure
0262: - resource debt and recovery states
0263: - build footprint and queue consequences
0264: - upgrade, repair, and tech timing tradeoffs
0265: - reward choices that alter the next day’s planning space
0266: - partial M1 UI wiring, which currently changes how clearly players can read state even when the rules already exist
0268: ### Win/Loss Conditions
0270: #### Victory Conditions
0272: - Survive through the end of `Day 15`
0273: - Maintain castle survival until the final night is fully resolved
0274: - Complete the full Day/Night loop without triggering the only hard failure condition
0276: #### Failure Conditions
0278: - `Castle HP reaches 0`
0279: - This is the only true run-ending failure condition in the locked PRD
0280: - Debt, pressure, and lost tempo increase failure risk, but do not themselves create separate defeat states
0282: #### Failure Recovery
0284: - On defeat, the player is shown the battle report and then returns to the main menu
0285: - Failure is intended to teach which planning decisions broke down:
0286: - underbuilt defenses
0287: - weak economy timing
0288: - poor queue or upgrade sequencing
0289: - inability to absorb late-night pressure
0290: - In the current M1 state, this learning loop is partially limited by incomplete outcome, prompt, and summary surfaces
0294: ## Game Mechanics
0296: ### Primary Mechanics
0298: #### 1. Build and Place Defenses
0299: - **What the player does:** Place structures during daytime using footprint and pathing rules
0300: - **When used:** Constantly during the preparation phase
0301: - **What it tests:** Planning, spatial judgment, lane prioritization, future-risk management
0302: - **How it feels:** Deliberate, grid-aware, consequence-heavy
0303: - **How it progresses:** More meaningful as the player unlocks better structures, understands lane pressure, and learns where placement mistakes cascade into night failures
0304: - **Pillars served:** Prepared Survival Over Reactive Chaos; Strategic Tradeoffs Across a 15-Day Arc
0306: #### 2. Manage Economy and Debt
0307: - **What the player does:** Spend, save, recover, and sequence actions under gold and iron constraints, including debt rules
0308: - **When used:** Constantly during daytime and indirectly across the full run
0309: - **What it tests:** Resource prioritization, discipline, sequencing, risk tolerance
0310: - **How it feels:** Tight, restrictive, always slightly pressured
0311: - **How it progresses:** The player gains mastery not by earning infinite resources, but by learning when to spend, pause, or accept short-term weakness
0312: - **Pillars served:** Deterministic Pressure With Legible Consequences; Strategic Tradeoffs Across a 15-Day Arc
0314: #### 3. Queue, Recruit, Upgrade, and Repair
0315: - **What the player does:** Manage training queues, building upgrades, and repairs inside limited timing and cost windows
0316: - **When used:** Frequently during daytime and between pressure spikes
0317: - **What it tests:** Sequencing, anticipation, opportunity cost judgment
0318: - **How it feels:** Structured, system-heavy, timing-sensitive
0319: - **How it progresses:** Complexity rises as more concurrent needs compete for the same economy and preparation window
0320: - **Pillars served:** Prepared Survival Over Reactive Chaos; Strategic Tradeoffs Across a 15-Day Arc
0322: #### 4. Read and Respond to Night Pressure
0323: - **What the player does:** Observe HUD state, wave pressure, lane escalation, damage intake, and defense performance during night combat
0324: - **When used:** Constantly during nighttime
0325: - **What it tests:** Situational reading, anticipation, interpretation of consequences rather than direct twitch execution
0326: - **How it feels:** Tense, validating, constrained
0327: - **How it progresses:** Nights become harder through budget scaling, elite/boss structure, and compounding earlier decisions
0328: - **Pillars served:** Deterministic Pressure With Legible Consequences; Readable Systems Before Spectacle
0330: #### 5. Choose Rewards and Progression Direction
0331: - **What the player does:** Resolve nightly reward choices, guide build direction, and shape future planning through progression options
0332: - **When used:** Once per night at a meaningful decision point
0333: - **What it tests:** Long-term planning, adaptation, valuation under uncertainty
0334: - **How it feels:** Relieving but strategic, never free of consequence
0335: - **How it progresses:** Choices become more meaningful as the run matures and existing weaknesses or strengths compound
0336: - **Pillars served:** Strategic Tradeoffs Across a 15-Day Arc; Prepared Survival Over Reactive Chaos
0338: ### Mechanic Interactions
0340: These mechanics are not independent. They form a dependency chain:
0342: - economy determines whether building, upgrading, training, and repairing are possible
0343: - building placement changes lane pressure, target resolution, and survivability at night
0344: - queue and upgrade timing compete with construction and repair for the same resource window
0345: - nighttime outcomes feed settlement and reward choices
0346: - reward choices influence the next day’s economy, defense priorities, and progression direction
0347: - incomplete M1 surfaces currently weaken readability, but the underlying interaction chain already exists
0349: In design terms, the game is strongest when the player can clearly see:
0350: `resource decision -> preparation outcome -> night pressure result -> reward/progression consequence`
0352: ### Mechanic Progression
0354: Mechanics evolve less through action-combo complexity and more through compounded system pressure:
0356: - early days teach basic building, recruitment, and survival rhythm
0357: - mid-run introduces stronger pressure from scaling waves, economy stress, and higher-value tradeoffs
0358: - late-run tests whether the player built a resilient system rather than a fragile short-term setup
0359: - elite and boss nights act as validation spikes for earlier choices
0360: - in the current M1 stage, progression understanding is partially limited by incomplete runtime presentation panels, not by missing systemic depth

1169: ### Gameplay Metrics
1173: #### Key Gameplay KPIs
1175: | Metric | Target | Measurement Method |
1177: | First-Night Survival Clarity | 新玩家在最小 onboarding 后能理解第一晚为何成功或失败 | Guided playtest observation + structured post-run interview |
1178: | Day/Night Loop Legibility | 多数测试玩家能正确描述白天在准备什么、夜晚在验证什么 | Playtest interviews using pillar language |
1179: | Day1-Day15 Run Completion at Intended Skill Bands | 低难可上手，高难可硬核；通关率符合 PRD 难度梯度预期 | Difficulty-band playtest cohorts + seeded run analysis |
1180: | Difficulty Usage Distribution | 玩家不会异常集中在单一难度，难度梯度有真实区分度 | Save/report telemetry and playtest tracking |
1181: | Economy Decision Meaningfulness | 玩家能感知债务、升级、训练、修理之间的真实取舍 | Post-session surveys + observation of repeated misplays |
1182: | Combat Fairness Perception | 玩家普遍将失败归因为决策不足而非随机不公 | Defeat interviews + battle report review |
1183: | Reward Choice Relevance | 奖励选择被视为能改变下一天 planning 的重要节点 | Reward selection logs + post-run recall |
1184: | UI Readability Closure | 测试玩家能通过 surface 直接读懂状态，而不是靠外部解释 | UI-focused playtests on HUD / outcome / economy / meta surfaces |
1185: | Battle Report Usefulness | 玩家或测试者能用战报解释 run 的关键失误或成功点 | Battle report review sessions |
1186: | Replayability Through Strategy Variation | 不同 build / reward / difficulty 路径能产生不同 run 体验 | Comparative seeded-run playtests |
